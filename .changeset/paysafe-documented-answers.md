Map more of Paysafe's documented error codes, which had fallen back to `card_declined` (the 402s) or `invalid_request` (3002 and 3005, 400s): 3039 and 3060 are a non-retryable `authentication_required`, 3016, 3054, 4001 and 4002 `fraud_suspected`, 3002, 3005, 3007, 3012 and 3019 `invalid_card_data`, the state checks 3202, 3205, 3403 and 3405 and the refunds the merchant account cannot fund (3412, 3413) `invalid_request`, the closed-account and unconfigured-card-type declines 3073 and 3008 `card_declined`, and 3416, 3418, 3419, 3503, 3504 and 3507, which refuse an operation the transaction or the account does not support, a non-retryable `unsupported_operation`, while a 429 or a 5xx is now always the retryable `rate_limited` or `psp_unavailable`, whatever code it carries. An expired refund and a verification in `ERROR` now report `failed`, a settlement that expired or that Paysafe filed with an error no longer counts as captured or refunded money nor gets refunded against, and a refund of a SEPA or Bacs payment, which Paysafe does not support, rejects with a non-retryable `unsupported_operation` before any refund request. `createdAt` and `capturedAt` now come out as normalized ISO 8601, including a settlement time Paysafe sends as epoch milliseconds, card expiry months and years sent as strings are read (`PaysafeCardLike.cardExpiry` and the `txnTime` of `PaysafePaymentLike.settlements` entries now allow those forms; TypeScript code reading them as a plain number or string must handle both), and Maestro (`MD`) and Solo (`SO`) cards report the brands `maestro` and `solo`.
